## Resolution

Findings captured at [`wayfinder/research/benchmark-corpus.md`](../research/benchmark-corpus.md): 71 cited initiatives across 13 companies (Netflix, Atlassian, Airbnb, Google, Microsoft, Canva, plus Spotify, Stripe, GitHub, Shopify, Basecamp/37signals, HashiCorp, Figma), grouped by 8 signals — Autonomy & Ownership, Growth & Mastery, Recognition, Cross-Team Visibility, Psychological Safety, Belonging & Inclusion, Career Progression Clarity, Purpose & Impact Visibility. Every entry cites sources; unverifiable claims are marked "no public evidence found" rather than invented.

Notable gaps flagged in the file: Canva has the thinnest public documentation of the required six companies; Google's 20% time and TGIF all-hands are noted as eroded, not thriving; Spotify's Squad Model includes its own public walkback rather than being presented uncritically; Figma has no public hack-week, mentorship program, or engineering career ladder.

Ready to ground ticket 004 (quality rubric) and tickets 006/007 (generation approach).

## Question

What are real, documented engagement/culture initiatives from Netflix, Atlassian, Airbnb, Google, Microsoft, and Canva (and comparable top-culture tech companies) that go beyond generic perks — specifically ones aimed at software engineers, architects, data scientists, designers, and product people? For each, capture: what problem/signal it addresses (autonomy, growth, recognition, cross-team visibility, psychological safety, etc.), how it's structured (cadence, format, resourcing, sponsorship level), and any evidence of impact. This corpus grounds both the quality rubric (ticket 004) and the generation-approach decision (ticket 006/007) — findings should be captured as a structured, tagged list, not prose, so later tickets can reference specific entries.
